4Postgres (managed)

The standard relational database: tables of rows that many users can change at once without corrupting anything. Your source of truth.

Think of it as

A shared spreadsheet that enforces its own rules.

Use it for

OrdersPaymentsStockBookings
Scenario. Two customers click the 9 AM Saturday slot at the tire shop at the same instant.
Without it
  1. Booking saved, but the bay never marked taken (crash in between)
  2. Both customers get the same bay
With it
  1. One transaction: mark bay taken and save booking, both or neither
  2. Unique rule on (bay, time) rejects the second insert
  3. Second customer is offered the next free slot
Why it worksThese facts must never be wrong, and the database guarantees them. A few writes a second is nothing for one database.
"Postgres, not DynamoDB: my write changes two rows together, and one database is plenty at these numbers."

Related terms

TransactionSeveral changes succeed or fail together (ACID).
ConstraintA rule the database enforces, like unique (bay, time).
IndexA sorted lookup, so a query doesn't read every row. Every frequent query needs one.
ManagedThe cloud handles backups, patches and recovery.
DynamoDBHuge-scale lookups by id; clunky multi-row changes; ~1,000 writes/s per hot item.
ShardingSplitting data across databases. Never in V1.
